Official constraints remain Qloo agentic integration, public functioning demo/source, four equal criteria, October 30 23:45 EDT deadline. ## Ranked investigation priorities

1. **Demand-first event formation — conditional next test.** Help an existing community organiser discover a feasible event its members would explicitly choose, including culturally related options they did not think to nominate. This moves candidate discovery before commitment and operational planning. It is not evidence that Qloo predicts demand. Strongest objection: direct nomination, voting or an equally informed LLM may supply equally good options, and Timeleft/Catalyst already demonstrate related formation mechanisms.
2. **Programme allocation — fallback only if the user need is observed.** A community coordinator allocates several sessions across limited slots/hosts while making unresolved member preferences visible. Strong technical structure, weaker sponsor necessity: known explicit preferences can feed a solver without Qloo, and Allocio already markets preference allocation. Carry over clear coverage reporting and infeasibility explanations rather than claiming scheduling is novel.
3. **Soundtrack Rescue — rejected non-event challenger.** A video editor replaces unusable reference music with suitable cleared alternatives that fit the edit. The pain is supported by professional music-supervision responsibilities, but Artlist, Soundstripe and Epidemic already document core mechanisms. Qloo artist coverage in an authorised catalogue and track-level value remain unproved. Do not pivot here just to be different.

Standalone event-rescue is not a fourth candidate: it is a reliability feature for the first two. It is testable engineering, not a sufficient originality thesis. There is currently no candidate that passes both demonstrated unmet need and essential-Qloo gates.

## Candidate 1: ask people before planning for them

- **User/pain:** organiser of an existing adult creative/social group selecting its next gathering. The testable hypothesis is that members' stated interests support a useful unfamiliar activity that nominations and generic suggestions miss. Existence/prevalence of this precise unmet need is not established.
- **Mechanism:** voluntary cultural anchors plus availability/budget → Qloo-grounded candidate discovery → participants approve specific feasible activities → planner selects a feasible programme from those approvals → operator reviews before any commitment.
- **Sponsor:** actual supported entity resolution and recommendation/ranking calls. Every candidate retains source IDs, input anchors and fallback status. User approval is the demand signal; cultural affinity only suggests candidates to evaluate. Event concepts themselves need explicit mapping to supported entities/tags; custom event names are not automatically Qloo entities.
- **Hard problem:** integrate incomplete taste evidence, a changing feasible catalogue and explicit consent without letting inferred affinity override real availability, vetoes or acceptance. Keep proposal versions, evidence and approvals consistent under revisions. Compare small exact solutions against a deterministic checker.
- **Accessible inputs:** fixture venue/host/slot catalogues for engineering checks; voluntary real group preferences for usefulness. Public venue listings do not prove availability. Group and operator participation are dependencies, not assumed assets.
- **Opening:** show members who have not approved any currently feasible idea; introduce a culturally grounded alternative, collect real choices, and show exactly whether the gap closes. Never animate invented responses as observed demand.
- **Visible change:** idea shortlist becomes one explicitly accepted, feasible proposal; a host withdrawal either repairs it with renewed approval or returns an honest infeasible state.
- **Fatal assumption:** Qloo proposes valuable options absent from ordinary nomination and a tool-using LLM supplied equivalent data. Failure means the core sponsor rationale is unsupported.
- **Scope:** one group, up to 12 initial activity formats, three venue records, three host records, six slots; no payments, mass outreach, social feed or full marketplace.

## Candidate 2: programme choice with visible omissions

- **User/pain:** coordinator choosing several activities for an existing community under a fixed operational budget. Hypothesis: repeatedly choosing the most popular individual session leaves members with no acceptable feasible option. Local operator evidence is still required; generic fairness literature does not prove this customer need.
- **Mechanism:** collect per-person approval/availability → select a small programme satisfying slot/host/capacity/budget constraints → show who has no approved option and why → ask whether to solicit more options or revise limits.
- **Sponsor role:** discover additional cultural candidates before approvals. Do not add unrelated raw affinity scores across people or call model coverage attendance. Within-query ranks can guide discovery, but cannot establish interpersonal utility.
- **Technical core:** constrained set selection, allocation and minimal-change repair, with explicit policy priorities and exact checks on bounded cases.
- **Opening:** two popular sessions leave some consenting participants with no approved option; a different feasible programme changes that count. Use actual approvals or conspicuously labelled fixtures.
- **Fatal assumption:** catalogue expansion meaningfully improves approved coverage beyond direct nominations. If an ordinary solver with the same explicit preferences produces the same outcome, sponsor value has not been demonstrated.
- **Scope:** same bounded catalogue; no public-resource allocation, employment matching or sensitive demographic inference.

## Candidate 3: Soundtrack Rescue, retained as a rejected challenge

- **User/pain:** editor who cannot use a client's reference song.
- **Mechanism:** licence/usage eligibility plus timing constraints → audience-cultural ranking → auditionable alternatives.
- **Sponsor hypothesis:** cross-domain affinity between supplied creative interests and catalogue artists, distinct from sonic similarity. Artist-level affinity may be a poor proxy for track fit.
- **Opening:** unusable soundtrack becomes an auditionable alternative aligned to a specific edit point.
- **Data/access:** needs an authorised auditionable catalogue; public API access alone does not establish preview or commercial-use rights.
- **Fatal assumptions:** catalogue coverage, permissions, and cultural advantage after acoustic fit are held constant.
- **Decision:** rejected before implementation because primary competitors already cover its clear benefit. A reversal would require authorised catalogue access plus a specific unserved decision and blind superiority on that decision; no automatic fallback promotion.

## Pairwise decision

Demand-first beats allocation as the next investigation because the question is whether cultural discovery produces a new accepted option. Allocation can demonstrate engineering without using cultural intelligence. Allocation beats soundtrack rescue only as a scoped technical fallback adjacent to available prototype work, not as a proven opportunity. Demand-first beats soundtrack rescue on data access and less direct reference-replacement overlap, but still faces substantial group-formation competition.

The ranking is an allocation of research effort, not forecast win probability. If no genuine organiser wants the discovery step, reject the champion rather than adding features. If users need allocation but Qloo adds nothing, it may be a useful ordinary product while failing this hackathon thesis.

## Precommitted 48-hour proof plan

The clock starts when necessary key access and authorised voluntary participants exist. Without them, only engineering fixtures can be tested; mark usefulness and Qloo gates blocked by missing evidence. No outreach or paid work is newly authorised by this plan.

**Hours 0–8: coverage and reproducibility**

- Prepare ten fixed decision briefs and a bounded, explicitly sourced or owner-supplied operational catalogue. Separate fixture cases from real observations.
- Check Qloo resolution for known, missing and ambiguous anchors. Log request settings, matched IDs, source timestamps and failed/degraded status within account terms. Require at least three feasible, grounded candidate options for eight of ten briefs as a practical continuation gate, not a general coverage estimate.
- Freeze constraints and evaluation criteria before comparing models. Obtain actual group input only with consent and authorised recruitment.

**Hours 8–24: separate engineering from sponsor value**

Run four conditions with the same operational inputs, output format and evaluation budget:

1. Direct nominations/interest filters plus a shared exact constraint checker/solver.
2. A tool-using LLM with the same operational catalogue, explicit preferences and access to equivalent discovery inputs, excluding Qloo.
3. The same model/planner plus Qloo cultural tools.
4. The same Qloo condition with shuffled taste anchors, as a diagnostic negative control, not user-facing advice.

Evaluate discovery separately from final selection. First, compare equal-sized candidate sets for feasibility and blind approval. Then pool the union and use the same solver for all approved options to determine whether gains came from new useful options, not privileged solver access. Where comparing orchestration, let the baseline use the same solver and Qloo tools too; a baseline success is a result, not something to hide.

**Hours 24–40: correction and acceptance**

- Run fixed missing-data, lower-budget, unavailable-host and conflicting-approval cases. Require zero hard-constraint violations, zero invented confirmations and explicit renewed approval after material changes. Record manual corrections and tool cost/latency.
- Ask consenting real participants/organisers to assess anonymised feasible outputs without revealing their source. Each participant sees the same number and order-randomised options. Feedback counts as real only when obtained; Codex/Claude cannot impersonate users.
- Planning continuation threshold: Qloo-assisted output preferred in at least seven of ten distinct briefs, with concrete preference reasons attributable to cultural choice, and at least three briefs containing an accepted additional option not supplied by the equal-budget baselines. Small-sample thresholds justify another test, not statistical superiority or market fit.

**Hours 40–48: decide and show proof**

- **Pass to further build:** coverage, feasibility and preference gates all pass; show measured examples and remaining failures.
- **Downgrade:** engineering passes but Qloo preference/discovery gains do not; keep coordination utility, withdraw sponsor advantage claim.
- **Pivot:** participants value a different named decision; investigate it with evidence rather than rebranding the same workflow.
- **Kill:** necessary data/participants cannot be accessed, no one wants the workflow, or the same value survives without meaningful Qloo contribution. No automatic promotion of candidate 2 or 3.

No real attendance/revenue improvement can be inferred from these short tests. Actual event outcomes require actual events. Do not optimise a generated metric and call it user value.
