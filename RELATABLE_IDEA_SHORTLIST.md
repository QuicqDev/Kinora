## 1. Tuesday Fix — give your quiet Tuesday a reason to exist

**User and task:** an independent café owner selects an evening concept within their actual staffing, equipment, capacity and budget. The agent confirms the venue, obtains missing operating facts, ranks cultural themes using Qloo, composes a feasible format and repairs it after a rejection. Output is a reviewable event proposal, preparation checklist and draft invitation.

**Problem evidence:** [Toast's Q3 2024 data](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/2024-reservation-trends) reports quieter early-week reservations in its selected restaurant cohorts. This is historical cohort evidence, not a current universal café statistic. A [Square owner interview](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/why-customer-relationships-are-like-wine-a-qa-with-dig-wines) describes addressing a slow Monday with loyalty incentives. It supports the problem and also a competing remedy: a new theme may not be the bottleneck.

**Honest presentation:** open on an owner-provided or clearly labeled sample Tuesday slot. Show the proposed event, its cost assumptions, equipment and remaining checks. The owner selects “no extra staff”; the format changes while the cultural theme is reconsidered or preserved for an explicit reason. End on the invitation draft. Never animate fictitious customers filling seats or claim predicted attendance. Original generic assets suffice; film screening and public music playback require separately verified rights.

**Qloo and boundary:** resolve the place and rank cultural entities, or use owner-confirmed taste anchors when appropriate. Venue affinity is an aggregate association, not a census of regular customers. A curated event-format library supplies operational facts. Qloo does not supply budgets, permissions, attendance or proof of demand. Actual event-account entitlement, venue coverage and meaningful signal variation remain untested.

**Closest competition:** [Kimola](https://kimola.com/blog/meet-new-gpt-prompts) already generates event ideas from restaurant reviews. [Veeso's indexed primary page](https://veeso.ai/ai-design-tools/restaurant-slow-night-promotion) advertises slow-night concepts, staff plans and coordinated promotion assets. [Doreniva](https://www.doreniva.com/) advertises local opportunities, operational recommendations and campaigns. [GeoTaste](https://devpost.com/software/geotaste-your-agentic-qloo-taste-business-consultant) is historical broad Qloo business intelligence. Therefore event planning, constraint handling and attractive cards do not establish originality. The remaining hypothesis is useful cultural grounding within this task.

**Scope/test:** one city, a small set of confirmed venues, five audited discussion/craft/community formats, one constraint-repair cycle. After separate testing authorization, check ten ordinary venues; require eight correct resolutions without substituting famous venues to hide failure. Six consenting operators would compare plans with identical facts, authorized review data, formats and visual treatment: their own approach, an informed LLM and Qloo-assisted planning. Require at least four of six to prefer Qloo's cultural choice to the LLM, with no lower feasibility and zero invented operational facts. This is an exploratory decision threshold, not statistical validation. Different recommendations alone do not pass. If theme choice is not the actual difficulty, pivot or drop. A fallback owner-selected theme preserves planning utility but fails the sponsor-advantage claim.

## 2. Both Sides — one wedding, room for both families

**User and task:** a couple protects meaningful musical moments while accommodating several explicitly declared taste circles. Qloo proposes bridge artists; verified track metadata and couple/DJ approval establish usable choices. A late veto repairs optional parts of the running order without silently displacing protected moments.

**Problem evidence:** a [firsthand wedding-planning account](https://www.reddit.com/r/weddingplanning/comments/1rf46hy/mixing_very_different_styles_of_music/) explicitly worries that more vocal relatives will dominate the music. This supports a concrete incident, not prevalence. The retrieved page's relative dates are inconsistent with the agent's inferred February date, so no exact publication date is asserted.

**Presentation:** a labeled example has 35 requests on one side and five on the other. The couple locks important moments; a timeline shows protected blocks and newly approved connecting choices. A veto exposes what changes. Counts and timeline allocations are actual inputs/application rules, never Qloo-generated fairness percentages. Show acceptance only after a real choice, not as an invented reaction.

**Competition:** [CrowdCue](https://www.crowdcueai.com/) already sequences requests using host formulas and adapts the playlist; [AudioMuse](https://github.com/NeptuneHub/AudioMuse-AI) already finds sonic song paths. The consumer lane also verified [Hitbook](https://hitbook.io/dj-studio) priorities, sections and approvals; Codex's direct fetch failed, so that detail remains lane-verified. The remaining combination is narrow and unproved. A skilled multicultural DJ is a strong baseline.

**Sponsor/scope/test:** artist affinity is not audio similarity, lyrical suitability or cultural significance. Use user-declared interests without inferring ethnicity, separate within-person ranks, verified metadata and a limited lawful catalog. Start with a DJ handoff rather than playback. Six couples plus DJ review would compare anonymous candidate bridges from Qloo, an informed LLM and genre/popularity choices; require at least four couples to prefer the Qloo choices with no hard-rule violations. If ordinary request tools already solve the problem or Qloo adds no accepted bridges, drop. Never promise a fuller dance floor or improved relationships.

## 3. Two Doors — give customers a reason to visit both shops

**User and task:** a café and nearby complementary shop draft one reciprocal offer. The agent proposes a partner, gathers each owner's limits, drafts responsibilities and redemption rules, then repairs the offer when one rejects a discount or time commitment. Qloo proposes cultural relevance; owners determine actual compatibility and willingness.

**Evidence and competition:** the business lane retrieved [PuffCuff's collaboration account](https://thepuffcuff.com/blogs/news/puffcuff-invites-small-business-collabs-with-better-together-initiative); Codex could not independently fetch it. [Collabing's indexed primary page](https://www.collabingapp.com/) already advertises partnerships, collaboration and campaigns. [Square's operator examples](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/creative-community-event-ideas) show real collaborative/community formats. These establish a familiar practice, not an unmet software market. Veeso even describes a food-truck/brewery joint campaign. This is the most crowded option and adjacent to the earlier rejected SponsorFit.

**Presentation and test:** two storefront cards become a shared offer and two printable counter cards. Replace “discount” with an owner-approved limited-seat activity and show the changed obligations. No fabricated revenue, audience-overlap percentages or partner consent. Scope to one neighborhood and a small verified partner pool. With subsequent authorization, six owner pairs would compare Qloo pairings to nearest-complementary-business and equally informed LLM baselines. At least four pairs must prefer the Qloo option and both owners must find the draft workable. If only copy generation helps, Qloo is decorative. Draft-only fallback cannot count as sponsor success. No contacts or publishing are part of this research.

## Pairwise judgment and decision conditions

Tuesday Fix beats Both Sides on a clearer business consequence and more natural cross-domain Qloo role; Both Sides wins on emotion. That ordering reverses if venue coverage/usefulness fails and couples independently prefer Qloo's bridges. Tuesday Fix beats Two Doors because one operator can assess a useful result without a second business's agreement. Two Doors could reverse that if actual partners value its less obvious matching and the café concept fails. Both Sides beats Two Doors on a clearer conflict and more visible repair; evidence of a distinct valuable partner workflow could reverse it.

Codex's subjective forecasts, not measurements: Tuesday Fix **6/8/6/4 = 24/40**; Both Sides **5/8/5/4 = 22/40**; Two Doors **5/7/5/4 = 21/40**, ordered technology/design/impact/idea. Scores below seven reflect untested coverage, sponsor lift, narrow gaps and no measured user outcomes. High design scores judge the proposed first-use story, not an implemented interface. These totals cannot override failed originality or utility gates.

No automatic crown transfer: the runner-up must pass its own gates. The user has requested ideas and presentation, so these options are offered for consideration while their risks remain visible. No implementation, authenticated API testing, recruiting or publication occurred.
