## 1. Heritage Bridge

**User and painful moment.** A parent wants to share films and music from a chosen family culture, but their child's current favorites are elsewhere. Choosing the parent's childhood classics may not create interest. A [firsthand SBS essay](https://www.sbs.com.au/voices/article/language-school-was-a-breeze-compared-to-teaching-my-own-kids-arabic/wc66k220g), published June 2022, describes the struggle to pass on Arabic and the author's plan to find suitable YouTube shows. This establishes one real experience, not prevalence or demand for our proposed app.

**The product.** Parent and child choose familiar favorites, specify the cultural collection they want to explore, and get three possible entry points from real existing works. They select one to try together. Afterwards, record actual interest separately from difficulty understanding the language. Future suggestions follow explicit feedback. The family defines what it wants to share; the application does not infer ancestry or decide whether someone is sufficiently connected to a culture.

**Qloo is the central decision source.** Resolve favorite titles/artists, retrieve or rank eligible cultural works from those signals, and retain the actual returned provenance. The [official tool chain](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qloo/qloo-hackathon-kit/main/starter/mcp-client/README.md) documents resolution, recommendations and bounded ranking. A curated, source-checked pool avoids assuming that a single genre tag defines a culture. Films and artists require separate type-aware requests; do not treat their raw scores as a common scale. Specific-work language, content information and viewing availability come from independent sources and family review, not affinity.

Use API attribution only when actually returned. If unavailable, say which input set was used without inventing a causal thread from one favorite. Dislike exclusions and new confirmed likes can affect subsequent requests; neither is evidence that Qloo has learned a person-specific model. No claimed percentage of enjoyment or cultural authenticity.

**Removal test.** A web-equipped LLM can still find titles, compare themes, check factual metadata and write explanations. What disappears is Qloo's modeled audience-affinity evidence underlying the core choice. That is a structural sponsor role; whether it improves recommendations remains an empirical question. Distinctive data alone does not prove useful superiority.

**First ten seconds.** Three favorite covers sit on the left; a parent-selected cultural collection on the right. One action produces three real candidate cards between them. Open a card to see the factual work details, Qloo provenance and an official viewing/listening link. The familiar scene is “I want to share this part of my life, but don't know where to start.” Candidate titles must come from live results, not a scripted pairing backfilled with a story. A seeded preview must be plainly labeled.

**Presentation direction.** Two small collections connected by three candidate cards, warm family language, no chat wall or percentage compatibility meters. One clear action: choose something to try together. Parent-provided memories can accompany a chosen work, but are not generated as if factual. A future repeat loop could support family collections and community-curated entry points; those are extensions, not claims of an ecosystem already validated.

**Competitors.** [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/apps) and its [developer app listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_SG&id=org.commonsensemedia.mobile) already support family interests and preferences. [MovieMuse](https://www.movie-muse.com/) advertises blended family tastes; that claim was accessible through indexed primary-page text, while direct extraction was empty. [Akademino](https://akademino.com/) advertises heritage-language stories and early access, also primarily indexed text. Personalization, family choice and heritage products all exist. The proposed difference is starting with cross-domain favorites to choose real works from an explicitly selected cultural collection. No assertion that competitors cannot support that exact flow.

**Technical scope.** One cultural collection, roughly 40 verified works, adult-controlled input, identity ambiguity handling, bounded multi-domain retrieval, eligibility checks, honest evidence display, and feedback-driven re-querying. Rights-cleared media embeds or official link-outs; no copied streaming media or review datasets. No new marketplace or payment system. Depth must come from reliable cultural retrieval and a reproducible comparison, not agent count.

**Strongest objection.** The family or a capable LLM may already choose equally appealing works. Regional entity coverage may be weak, especially within appropriate works. A content rating is not a suitability guarantee. A parent's confidence about what a child would like is not actual child interest.

## 2. Hooked

**Pitch:** Find the Spanish show or artist you would choose for fun, starting with favorites you already know.

An adult learner has little target-language taste history. Qloo supplies cross-domain affinity; separate metadata establishes actual work language, and explicit feedback distinguishes enjoyment from comprehension. The ten-second transformation is familiar favorites becoming one concrete native-language first encounter. No promised proficiency gain or inferred difficulty percentage.

A [2023 peer-reviewed framework](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/selecting-and-using-authentic-videos-for-intentional-second-langu/) discusses multiple considerations when selecting authentic learning videos. It does not validate our method. [Lingopie's own help](https://help.lingopie.com/support/solutions/articles/150000076261-help-choosing-a-show) already offers level/genre filters and personalized assistance. [LingQ](https://www.lingq.com/en/?landing=false) provides interest-led media and importing. These are real baseline products, not straw opponents.

Qloo is central to the choice but its advantage may be marginal. Scope one language with verified shows/artists, official previews/link-outs and two feedback questions. First test uses identical candidate pools and cards across Qloo, LLM-with-web, genre and popularity. Measure voluntary choice, actual first encounter and desire to continue, with comprehension separately reported. This is a cleaner adult testing route but a less distinctive product story than Heritage Bridge.

## 3. Dub First — do not advance

Choose which two culture-commentary episodes deserve a professionally localized pilot. [YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en-GB) recommends focused language investment and performance analysis. But [Unilingo](https://www.unilingo.co/pricing) already advertises machine-learning video selection using public performance, private channel statistics and target-language search. Qloo interest in a video's subjects is not evidence of interest in that creator's treatment. The geographic evidence-to-video-performance gap remains too large for this claim. No revenue or view forecasts are justified. The useful transferable idea is a small controlled rollout, not this proposed product.

## Falsification and crown transfer

Proposed 48-hour protocol after the appropriate access and study authorization:

1. Freeze a 40-work first cultural collection with original-language and content sources. Predeclare correct-resolution target of 32/40; report all misses and selection bias rather than silently replacing failures. Verify bounded ranking and attribution behavior. Independently check suggestions against the chosen collection constraints.
2. With eight consenting families, compare Qloo and an equally informed LLM with web access using identical pool, facts, presentation and limits. Include the parent's unaided choices. Conceal model identity. Proposed exploratory continuation gate: at least six families prefer the Qloo suggestions, with ties not counted as wins. This gate is a small-study decision rule, not statistical proof.
3. Parent preference only supports parent usefulness. Observe voluntary child interest and an actual shared first encounter before claiming the proposed family benefit. Do not substitute fictional personas or model-generated preferences for this evidence. Measure effort too.
4. Use shuffled favorite signals as a diagnostic, not a success metric. More varied outputs do not necessarily mean better choices.
5. Missing attribution downgrades explanation features. Poor regional coverage kills the claimed launch scope; do not hide it by declaring another culture equivalent. Hooked only takes the lead if it independently passes its adult-language coverage and usefulness test. It is not an automatic fallback just because its catalogue is easier.

If no useful advantage appears over the strongest baseline, retire the claimed Qloo advantage. No need to block concept research solely on the missing key. No implementation, API call, recruitment, purchase or publication occurred in this hunt.
